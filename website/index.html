The whole pathway

From a head full of noise
to a scheduled day

Every step below is a real thing the app does, in the order it does it. Nothing here needs you to organise anything first, which is the entire point.

1

You open the app and hold the orb

No blank list, no empty calendar demanding to be filled in. Home opens on the orb, and tapping it starts recording immediately. The bar to entry is one tap, because a bar any higher is a bar you will not clear on a bad day.

Home tab
2

You talk, uninterrupted, until you tap done

The mic stays open the entire time. No pause detection, no silence timeout, no auto-stop when you stop to think. Ramble, contradict yourself, trail off and come back. The orb swells with your voice so you can see it is still listening, and the recording only ends when you say it does.

Voice dump sheet
3

The whole recording becomes text

The full audio goes to Whisper and comes back as a transcript. Not a live-typing guess that drops words when the signal wobbles, but the complete thing, transcribed once at the end, so nothing you said gets lost mid-sentence.

Speech service
4

It gets broken into separate, doable tasks

The transcript runs through the same extraction the typed Brain Dump uses. "I need to email the landlord and I should probably tidy up and mom keeps texting" comes back as three distinct tasks, each one small enough to actually start, phrased as an action rather than an anxiety.

Brain dump extraction
5

Each task is tagged with what it costs you

Every task gets an energy level: high for the things that need a working brain, low for the ones you can do while distracted, dead for the ones you can do at your worst. This is the single idea the whole app is built on, because a to-do list that treats "file taxes" and "take meds" as equal work is a list that fails you by lunchtime.

Energy model
6

Auto-schedule lays them across the real day

It knows your sleep window and roughly when you crash. High-energy tasks land in the hours you actually have, dead-brain tasks get parked in the dead-brain hours, and nothing gets scheduled into the time you are asleep. Drag anything and the rest reflows around it.

Schedule tab
7

Reminders fire on your phone, not just in the app

Real system notifications at the scheduled time, so the plan reaches you even when the app is closed and forgotten, which is exactly when a plan needs to reach you.

Reminders
8

You start it, with someone there if that helps

Pull the task straight into the focus timer, or join a live body-doubling room and work next to people who are also trying to begin. Nobody talks. That is the feature.

Focus tab
9

It remembers, and tomorrow starts smarter

What you dumped, what you finished, what you moved four times, and how you felt while doing it all get kept. Mynd re-reads them and writes notes back to you: you start better at 9:30 than 8:00, the report is an opening problem. The coach reads the same picture, so "I'm stuck" gets an answer about your actual life.

Usage

What every screen is for

The app has four tabs and a handful of surfaces behind them. Here is what each one does, and when you would actually open it.

Home

Where you land. The orb, today's count, the brain dump box, what to do now, your progress bars, mood, and goals. Open it when your head is full and you need to put it somewhere.

Tab 1 · today

Schedule

The day as a timeline, with each task in the hour that suits its energy. Auto-Schedule builds it, you drag to override. Open it when you want to know what is next rather than everything at once.

Tab 2 · schedule

Focus

A timer bound to one task, your done-today and focused-percentage counts, and the list of live body-doubling rooms. Open it at the moment you have decided to start and need to not stop.

Tab 3 · focus

Coach

Chat with Mynd, by text or by voice. It has your context, so it answers about your actual week. Four one-tap prompts cover the usual needs. Open it when you are stuck and cannot name why.

Tab 4 · chat

Voice dump

The sheet that opens when you tap the orb. Records until you tap done, then transcribes and breaks down. Open it when typing is too much friction, which is most of the time.

From the Home orb

Memory

Notes Mynd wrote about you, your own words kept verbatim, and parked thoughts. Open it when you want to know what the app thinks it knows, or to retrieve something you set down weeks ago.

From Home

Community

An anonymous feed, groups, direct chat, and the focus rooms other people have opened. Open it when the problem is that you feel like the only person who works this way.

From Focus

Reminders

System notifications tied to scheduled tasks, plus your sleep window so nothing gets planned into the hours you are asleep. Set once, then forget it exists.

From Profile

Profile

Your streak calendar, your anonymous handle, low-stim mode, theme, sleep schedule, and your subscription. Open it rarely, which is the intention.
